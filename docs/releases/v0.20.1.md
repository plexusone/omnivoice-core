# v0.20.1

**Release date:** 2026-11-18

This release fixes cases where `stt/verify` reported a quotation as
disputed although every engine heard the same words, and one case where a
quotation with an elision produced a clip covering most of the recording.
They were found by checking a podcast case study's quotations against the
episode's audio.

## Fixed

### Spoken forms and word boundaries (`stt/verify/`)

Engines write the same speech differently. These differences no longer
count as disagreements:

- **Informal forms.** "gonna", "wanna", "gotta", "kinda", "sorta" and "ok"
  normalize to "going to", "want to", "got to", "kind of", "sort of" and
  "okay".
- **A word split in two.** Two heard words are joined when together they
  spell a quoted word, now also when the first is itself a quoted word:
  "a synchronous" is compared as "asynchronous". They stay apart when both
  are quoted words on their own.
- **Two words run together.** A heard word that spells two quoted words in
  a row is split: "speedups" is compared as "speed ups". It stays whole
  when it is itself a quoted word or could split two ways.
- **Broken-off words.** A word cut off as the speaker restarted, written
  with a trailing hyphen ("at ha- at heart"), is dropped unless every token
  it yields is a quoted word ("run out of- context" keeps "of"; "kinda-" is
  dropped whole). A broken-off word is never joined to another word or
  counted as part of a number ("twenty five-" is 20).
- **Locating.** Finding a quotation in the timed transcript applies the
  same rules, so a quoted "speed ups" is found where the transcript has
  "speedups".

### Elisions (`stt/verify/`)

Words heard across a marked elision ("…") used to cost nothing in the
alignment. A quotation with an elision could then match a stretch running
on to a later repeat of its last words, minutes away; its clip covered most
of the recording and exceeded an engine's upload limit. Alignments now
compare by edits first and by words skipped at elisions second: among
matches with the same edits, the shortest stretch wins, and a later exact
match still beats a nearer match with a misheard word.

### Alignment (`stt/verify/`)

Costs are 64-bit, so aligning a very long reference against an empty
hearing returns one deletion per token instead of panicking.
